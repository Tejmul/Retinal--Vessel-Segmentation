clDice buys connectivity
and pays in precision

We added the centre-line Dice loss to Study Group Learning to stop the network breaking thin capillaries. It worked — 31% fewer broken branches, a third as much fragmentation, on all 20 test images. It also cost 7.6 points of F1. The hypothesis predicted the opposite.

The hypothesis, scored

The literature review committed to three numbers before any training ran. Here they are against row D minus row A.

All three fail, and two fail in the direction the review did not consider: clDice made the model less precise, not more sensitive-and-precise. On a 20-image test set the review set a one-point F1 noise floor; the 7.6-point drop clears it by a wide margin, and it is the same sign on 20 of 20 images, so it is not sampling noise.

What it actually bought

Aggregate F1 hides the mechanism. Splitting the label by vessel calibre — a pixel within 1.5px of the background sits in a vessel about three pixels across — shows where each model spends its errors.

Broken branches are connected runs of the label's skeleton that the prediction fails to cover. Thin/thick sensitivity splits recall by the label's distance transform.

The trade is legible

clDice does what it promises. Broken branches fall from 141 to 97, fragmentation from 57 components to 20, and thin-vessel recall is the best of the four rows. Thick vessels were never the problem — every row already finds them at 98%+.

The bill arrives as false positives: the rate nearly doubles, 0.043 to 0.079. A loss that only rewards covering the label's centre-line has no reason to keep the prediction thin, so the model thickens everything until the skeleton is covered. That is what costs the F1, and it is visible in the loupe as red fringe along true vessels.

Reading it honestly

Two findings here are worth more than the headline.

SGL did not help under this budget. Row A against row B is −1.6 points of F1 and −0.7 of accuracy. The pseudo-labels bought +7.0 points of sensitivity and +2.0 of clDice, so they shift the operating point rather than dominating. The review suspected this and asked for the control row; the control earned its place.

α = 0.5 is almost certainly too strong. clDice was given equal weight with cross-entropy because the hypothesis said "an equal mix". The connectivity gain saturates while the precision loss does not, so the interesting experiment is a sweep from 0.1 to 0.3 — which this run does not cover and should not be read as covering.

One check on the reproduction: the paper's published drive_k8 weights load into this re-implementation with all 324 tensors matching, and score AUC 0.9886 / F1 0.8316 against the paper's reported ≈0.983 / ≈0.82. The architecture and evaluation protocol are right; the retrained rows sit lower because they get 30 epochs at 128px rather than the paper's longer schedule at 256px.
